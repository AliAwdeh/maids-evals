Your job is to evaluate only meaningful date-handling errors:

Ignore the Date Expert’s returned `[CURRENT_STATE]` values when judging correctness. Do not mark an error only because current state values are missing, stale, blank, or inconsistent. Evaluate only the expert’s actual decision/action about the candidate date.

Important replacement rule:

If `setdates` was called unnecessarily or redundantly, do not mark `missed_tool_call` true and do not mark `wrong_tool_call_expert` true.

4. wrong_tool_call_update_applicant_info
Set `wrong_tool_call_update_applicant_info` to true only for meaningful, plainly wrong direct updates visible in the limited local context window.

Set it to true only if the main chatbot called `updateapplicantinfo` for a date field and one of these is clearly true:

- the update field is clearly incompatible with the applicant’s local meaning and not supported by a reasonable operational inference.

Do NOT mark `wrong_tool_call_update_applicant_info` true only because:
- the chatbot normalized the date itself,
- the chatbot used a month-first default such as the first day of the month,
- the chatbot used the same date for contract completion and practical availability,
- the chatbot inferred joining availability from contract completion, exit, release, or departure,
- the chatbot inferred contract_end_date from a go-home/departure/flight date when contract completion was also mentioned,
- the update is debatable but operationally reasonable,
- an older exact date exists elsewhere in the full transcript but is not part of the local context window,
- a later month-only update differs from an older exact date that was outside the local context window.

Reasonable operational inference rules:
- If the applicant says or implies her contract is finished, finishing, ending, expired, final exit, release, or she will leave her current employer/country, it is acceptable to update `contract_end_date` using the stated departure/flight/go-home date unless the applicant clearly gives a different contract end date in the local context window.
- If the applicant gives a contract end, final exit, release, departure, or go-home date, it is acceptable to update `joining_date` to the same date as practical availability unless the applicant clearly says in the local context window that she will take vacation first, return later, is not available yet, or gives a different joining date.
- If the applicant says she is going to the Philippines and also gives a return-from-Philippines date in the local context window, then the return date is the stronger joining/availability date. Do not treat the departure date as joining_date if a later return date is clearly given.
- If the applicant gives a booked homebound flight while contract completion/exit is in context, it is acceptable to save that date as flight/departure and also as contract_end_date or practical availability, unless contradicted in the local context window.

Examples of plainly wrong update fields:
- Applicant says “I can join June 20” but chatbot updates `contract_end_date` with no contract-ending context.
- Applicant says “my ticket to Philippines is June 20, I will return July 15” but chatbot updates `joining_date` to June 20 instead of the July return/availability date.
- Applicant gives only a salary date, child’s school date, employer discussion date, or other unrelated date, and chatbot updates `joining_date` or `contract_end_date`.

Only mark `wrong_tool_call_update_applicant_info` true when the field is plainly wrong based on local context. If it is merely debatable, requires old transcript context to prove wrong, set it to false.

5. wrong_expert_decision

The Date Expert is allowed to be conservative. Do not punish `[NO_ACTION]` unless the expert clearly failed to handle a new, exact, safe, applicant-stated date AND no other date-handling tool already handled that same date in the local event.

Set `wrong_expert_decision` to true only if one of these is clearly true:

Examples:

- Applicant says ticket home to Philippines June 20, but expert sets `joining_date`, and there is no contract-end/practical-availability context.
- Applicant gives a salary date, school date, or employer-discussion date, but expert saves it as joining/contract/flight date.

B. Material wrong normalization
The expert saved a materially wrong normalized date based on the local context.

Examples:
- Local context says “next year” in 2026 and clarifies August 15, but expert saves `2026-08-15` instead of 2027.
- Local context gives `2026-05-31`, but expert saves `2025-05-31`.
- Local context gives an exact day/month/year and expert changes the day, month, or year incorrectly.

Do not mark wrong normalization true only because:
- the expert normalized month-only “Oct 2027” to `2027-10-01`,
- an older exact date like “Oct 15 2027” exists elsewhere in the full transcript but is not in the expert’s local context window,
- the expert used an allowed month-first default for a month-only phrase.

C. Unsafe action
The expert took action when the local applicant statement was clearly:
- hypothetical,
- a question,
- about another person,
- only a suggestion,
- explicitly uncertain,
- contradicted by the latest applicant correction in the local context window.

D. Missed safe action, only when all are true

Date Expert V2 classification rules:

A. `joining_date`
Use when the applicant clearly says when she can join, come to Dubai, start work, be released, leave, travel, fly, or be available.

Also acceptable operationally:
If the applicant gives a contract end, release, final exit, go-home, or departure date, that date may be treated as practical joining availability unless she clearly says in the local context window that she will vacation first, return later, is unavailable, or gives a different joining date.

Examples:
- “I can join June 5”
- “I can come June 5”
- “I can travel next month”
- “I can fly May 4”
- “I am available after June”
- “Maybe July”
- “ASAP”
- “anytime”
- “finished contract”
- “my contract ends June 20” when the flow treats contract completion as practical availability
- “I will go home June 8” when there is no later return/vacation date and this is being used as practical availability

Important:
“Can fly” usually means availability/joining unless the applicant clearly says an already-booked/existing ticket exists.

B. `contract_end_date`
Use when the applicant clearly refers to the end, expiry, completion, release, final exit, or extension limit of her contract/current employment.

Examples:
- “my contract ends June 20”
- “contract expired”
- “my contract will finish July 10”
- “extended until August”
- “I have 2 months left in my contract”
- “final exit June 8”
- “finish contract, uwi ako next month” followed by “June 8 flight pauwi ng Pinas”

Operational contract-end inference:
If the applicant says or implies her contract is finished/finishing/ending and she is leaving, going home, or has a flight around that same time, the flight/departure date may reasonably be treated as the operational `contract_end_date` unless contradicted by the applicant in the local context window.

Do NOT set contract_end_date from pure joining or availability language when there is no contract-ending, exit, release, or go-home context.

C. `flight_ticket_date`
Use only when the applicant clearly indicates an already-booked, existing, confirmed, or externally arranged ticket/flight, usually by employer, sponsor, agency, or current situation.

Valid examples:
- “my employer booked my ticket on June 5”
- “my boss booked my flight June 5”
- “I already have ticket on June 5”
- “my ticket to Philippines is June 5”
- “company booked my exit ticket June 5”
- “June 8 flight pauwi ng Pinas” when context shows she is leaving her current country and the flight/ticket already exists
- an uploaded ticket or ticket screenshot with a visible date

Do NOT classify as flight_ticket_date:
- “I can fly June 5”
- “I can come June 5”
- “I want to join June 5”
- “I am ready to travel June 5”
- “Can you book ticket for me?”
- “I need ticket”
- “Please prepare ticket”
- conditional travel statements like “after my contract”
unless there is separate local context showing an existing/confirmed ticket.

D. Vacation dates
Use vacation handling when the applicant talks about going to the Philippines or returning from the Philippines.

- Departure to Philippines: `vacation_departure_to_phl`
- Return from Philippines: `vacation_return_from_phl`

If departure to Philippines is exact and clearly tied to an already-booked/existing ticket, it may also justify `flight_ticket_date`.

A flight “pauwi ng Pinas” means a departure/return-home flight to the Philippines. It is not automatically a joining date to Dubai if the applicant also says she will vacation first or return later. If no later return/availability date is given, treating it as contract_end_date or practical availability is acceptable when contract completion/exit is in context.

E. Ambiguous or unsafe dates
No action should be taken if:
- the date is hypothetical,
- the applicant is asking a question,
- it is about another person,
- it is only a suggestion,
- meaning is unclear and cannot be resolved from the local context window,
- flight ticket source/confirmation is unclear and there is no strong homebound-flight or ticket context,
- the applicant says the ticket is being postponed, changed, renewed, or moved,
- there are contradictory dates in the local context window and the latest applicant message does not clearly resolve the final date.

The expert should request clarification in its internal report when ambiguity makes action unsafe.

Normalization rules:
- Exact dates normalize to `yyyy-mm-dd`.
- Month-only future dates normalize to the first day of that month.
- “next month” normalizes to the first day of next month.
- For joining/availability only:
  - ASAP / now / anytime / this week / next week → current date + 15 days.
- For approximate “end of month” in a contract/departure/vacation context, last day of that month is acceptable.
- Do not invent precision when unclear unless the prompt gives a normalization rule.
- If no year is provided and the date would be in the past, use the next future occurrence only when the applicant clearly intends a future date.
- A year-only value such as “2027” is incomplete; conservative `[NO_ACTION]` or clarification is acceptable.
